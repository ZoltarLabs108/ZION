A different kind of edge

Why this letter leads with drawdown, not a price target.

Most market letters open with a call — a number, a direction, a target. This one opens with a distinction. ZION's big equity sleeves — S&P and Nasdaq — converge to roughly their own long-run drift, and we don't pretend the directional edge there is more than a fraction of a percent. The book's ballast doesn't need a forecast to work. But direction is not absent — it is rationed. On the roughly one-in-five months a certified filter fires, ZION takes a real, out-of-sample directional edge (§III). The rest of the time it engineers the ride and waits.

What ZION engineers underneath that is the shape of the ride — the second moment. A book of near-orthogonal sleeves, an always-on hedge, and a throttle that cuts exposure when market stress rises, assembled so that something is usually working when the rest is not. The claim is not that we predict everything. It is that we lose less, and less often, act on direction only where it survives out-of-sample, and can show you exactly how.

Loss-limiting every month by construction; directional only where a filter clears out-of-sample. Both are testable claims — and the live tape is now testing them.

VWhy it holds up in a crisis

Three mechanisms, none of which requires calling the top.

Decorrelation across cadence. ZION runs a weekly book and a monthly book, combined 50/50 with zero selection freedom. Their returns correlate just +0.20 — they make money in different months. The monthly sleeves even carried the years the weekly book was still in its gate burn-in (2007–2016), so the combined record has no dead decade.

An always-on hedge. The two-year-Treasury leg and gold overlay are not tactical bets that have to be right on timing — they are standing ballast that rises when risk assets bleed.

A stress throttle. A daily tail-risk overlay feeds a governor that cuts gross exposure as liquidity and volatility deteriorate, and takes the book flat under stress — mechanically, before a drawdown compounds.
